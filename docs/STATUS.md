# Current status and handoff

Updated 23 October 2026 AEDT. This is a working development build, not a completed production release or finished Yard submission.

## Implemented

- Version 0.2.0: universal browser activation with a one-time optional site-access grant, persistent tab/navigation/restart behavior, excluded sites and Pause everywhere. Chrome-protected pages remain unavailable.
- A toolbar popup holds everything that need not sit on the page: Grab selection, Grab page, Turn on everywhere / Pause everywhere, vault status (connected only while captures sync and no vault test has failed; a failed test, with Test vault; a saved key not syncing, because sync is paused or Chrome's access is off, with Test vault; or no vault, with Connect vault; and how many captures are waiting or kept on the device), Settings, the graveyard and gesture help. Opening it grants activeTab; its grab wakes the active tab and asks the page to take its selection or the whole page, and the popup stays open until the page has taken the capture, showing the page's reason when nothing was taken (no selection, paused, a refused target), then closes while the hand plays on. If Chrome's site-access prompt closes the popup mid-activation, the background finishes that activation when the grant arrives, but only for a request made in the last two minutes for access not already held; a popup that survived the prompt waits for that activation rather than reporting a failure.
- Trusted Shift/Control circle/oval capture that forgives a loop stopping short (from about 0.83 of a turn), spiralling in or out, overshooting, wobbling or drawn as an egg, while still refusing a half circle or a scribble back and forth. A loop takes what it goes round, in reading order (a column of steps, a list, search results, a picture), and a small circle one sentence (see The enclosing lasso); the preview highlights exactly what will be taken, source title/URL, early-release/typing/drag/focus cancellation and recoverable Spit back. Hovering alone never captures.
- One-click grabs: clicking the corner hand takes the current selection, otherwise the page: its title, link, byline (author, site, date) and article, read by Defuddle (the reader in Obsidian's Web Clipper) from a copy of the page without its scripts, its linked data's article text or the elements the browser does not render ([DEPENDENCIES](DEPENDENCIES.md) lists exactly what is left out), up to 32,000 characters and marked when cut short. When the reader finds too little, the earlier 4,000-character excerpt of visible article/main reading text is kept, skipping navigation, forms, dialogs, editable and hidden content.
- A passage carries the nearest visible heading above it, the paragraph around it when there is more of it, the page's byline and a text-fragment link that opens the page scrolled to it. Notes gain those fields (and `whole_page`, `truncated`) only when captured, so a note without them is written byte for byte as before.
- The graveyard wiki kit (`vault-kit/`, installed with `scripts/install-vault-kit.mjs`): an `AGENTS.md` schema, Claude Code settings and a hook that keep an agent to reading that folder and changing only `wiki/`, with no shell, web or other tools, even where the person's own settings allow more, and `/ingest`, `/ask` and `/lint` skills that compile the captures into linked, cited pages, following Andrej Karpathy's "LLM Wiki" pattern. The extension does not run the agent. A settled selection grows a 30px chip at its end. Alt+Shift+Z takes a selection wherever the pointer rests, otherwise the pointed target.
- The page overlay is the hand and one bar: idle, a bare 36px hand; during a capture, a one-line status to its left (the hand sits at the right end, so it never moves), with only the buttons the moment needs (Cancel, Spit back, and Connect vault when no vault is connected). An error adds one short detail line ("Kept on this device. / Connect a vault to bury it in Obsidian.") and goes quiet after 12 seconds; saves go quiet after six. A related note has its own small toggle. There is no ⋯ menu, panel or receipt card any more, and a page can no longer pause the hand everywhere. At capture time one of three filmed zombie hands (generated footage matted onto the page, picked at random and never the same twice running) punches through the page below the target and slaps flat. It closes its fist on a paper copy of the words in the page's own type and colours (or the page peeled into a card), which tears free with a ragged edge, lifts and crumples into a creased ball in the fist, and yanks it under on a confirmed save or lets go on failure. After a yank the wall mends: filmed plaster flies back into the hole and its cracks close. The footage plays at 1.2× the speed it was filmed, and its wall (rim, plaster, dust, the marks the fingers leave) takes the page's own background colour, so a dark page gets dark plaster rather than a pale glow. The hand scales with the page's text size. Reduced motion shows a static grip; delayed saves fade the decoration within 3.6 seconds while the status stays truthfully queued.
- Atomic local capture/journal, serialized vault writes, create-only files, ETag metadata updates, attachment acknowledgements, lost-response reconciliation, visible failure states and bounded retries. With no vault connected a capture is kept on the device and its grab still plays out: the hand pulls the copy under and the page heals, while the status says it is kept on this device rather than in the vault.
- Packaged local MiniLM inference, persisted vectors, semantic/keyword retrieval and related-note ghosts (only for a semantic match scoring at least 0.3). No AI provider key is required.
- Image bytes → verified visible crop → labelled link; explicit exact-origin download consent separate from universal page access, immediate revocation, no credential forwarding, size/type/redirect restrictions.
- YouTube confirmation-time moments, ad-clock rejection, partial native transcript reader, manual WebVTT/SRT/timestamped import, provenance and cue-specific search links.
- Recovery preserves pending local lifecycle changes, quarantines positively observed missing/edited evidence, and adopts verified newer remote lifecycle only when the local saved snapshot has not changed.
- Isolated local gaze calibration lab with explicit camera control, settled and steadiness-checked capture per point, per-session model choice by leave-one-target-out cross-validation, held-out diagnostics, a One Euro–smoothed live dot with a head-movement warning, a click-only diagnostics download with an offline evaluator, no automatic persistence and blocked external connections. Gaze does not arm page captures, and after one human session it is shelved: Settings no longer links to the lab, whose code and tests stay. See Gaze tuning below.
- Full and core build profiles, strict contracts, AST boundaries, pinned dependencies, public-source exclusions, license notices, CI and validated archive generation.

## Gaze tuning

**One human session has been measured, and gaze is shelved for this deadline.** On 21 October Sam ran the lab once in Chrome on macOS (a 1280×773 viewport at 2× pixel density) and `scripts/gaze-eval.mjs` evaluated its diagnostics, which reproduced every live capture. The five check targets were 43, 89, 96, 133 and 146 px off: a median of 96 px, about 7% of the viewport's width and 12% of its height. The lab chose iris + head rotation at λ 1, and every variant's checks fell between 86 and 113 px, so the model was not the limit. The errors were scatter, not one shared offset: correcting each check by the other four's mean offset left a median of 103 px, so a single recentring point would not help. Vertical was the weaker direction (three checks 75–131 px off vertically), and the bottom-left calibration point was captured on its timeout, the eyes having moved 0.009 eye widths from the point above it against the 0.02 limit. The constants below were the best of every sweep on this session: settling 0, 200 or 500 ms gave 116, 104 and 118 px; a 10-frame window 100 px; a tighter steadiness range left points uncaptured. Smoothed, the live dot still moved a median 22 px per update (90th percentile 92 px), and Sam judged it to wander too much to show. A paragraph is 100–200 px tall, so at this accuracy gaze could not choose one. Gaze is therefore cut from the demo and the submission, Settings no longer links to the lab, and its code, tests and evaluator stay for later work. The diagnostics file is not in the repository. This is one session, and the constants are unchanged. They live in `src/features/gaze/tuning.ts`:

- Collection: drop the first 350 ms after a target appears; capture once 8 consecutive frames keep the binocular iris position within a 0.03 eye-width range and that position lies at least 0.02 eye widths from where the eyes were in the target's first 150 ms, so a person still looking at the previous point is not captured there; reject frames beyond 3.5 robust deviations and average the rest; after 2.5 s, points that never became steady or never moved are captured and flagged rather than blocking. A shrinking centre dot is the fixation point; Click (or Space, since the captured target takes focus) enables only after capture. Optional auto-advance (default off) moves on 300 ms after capture.
- Model: leave-one-target-out cross-validation over variants iris-only, iris + quadratic, iris + head-rotation and all ten features, each with λ ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10} (targets weighted equally), choosing the simplest candidate within 10% of the best mean error; standardised inputs clipped to ±4.
- Live dot: One Euro filter in CSS pixels, min cutoff 0.6 Hz, β 0.0005, derivative cutoff 1 Hz, restart after a 500 ms gap. On synthetic white noise this leaves 0.40–0.48 of 40–120 px jitter and reaches 90% of a 600 px jump in ~320 ms.
- Movement warning: inter-eye distance ±12% or eye-centre shift > 0.5 inter-eye distances, cleared below 80% of each limit. Both are measured in the camera's normalised image coordinates, so on a 4:3 camera the shift limit is about a quarter tighter for vertical movement, and head roll beyond roughly 35° can trip the distance limit.
- Export and evaluation: **Download session diagnostics** (contents listed in [PRIVACY](PRIVACY.md)); `node scripts/gaze-eval.mjs <file…>` reprints per-variant cross-validation and check errors, an approximate pre-tuning reference, how steady and how far moved the live captures were, and settling, window-length and steadiness-range sweeps.

Synthetic evidence only: unit tests show the selection picks the quadratic variant for a quadratic map, iris-only when head features are noise, and iris + head when head turns explain the gaze; with the eye arriving 250, 750 or 1200 ms after each target appears, the synthetic check median is 3–7 px across three seeds, and stays 3–7 px with settling removed, because the steadiness range alone keeps reaction-period frames out of these step-like synthetic saccades (real saccades overshoot and drift, which settling still covers). Regression tests cover late arrival and a person who never leaves the previous point. On synthetic eyes, which never glance away after capture, the approximate pre-tuning reference with 12 averaged frames can match or beat the 8-frame window; on the one human session the 10-frame window gave 100 px against the 8-frame window's 96, and a 12-frame window captured only 1 of 14 points.

Live inspection of one public YouTube watch page found its native transcript panel stuck on a spinner with no exposed cues. That is an extraction/availability limitation, not a claim that captions are absent. No broad native-transcript success rate is established; manual import is implemented and browser-tested.

## Wiki and note syntax (21 October, after the capture follow-ups)

The kit's hook kept the agent's writes inside `wiki/` but did not look at what they said, and Obsidian does more with some text than show it. It hides `%%` and HTML comments; it fetches a Markdown image or an embed of a web address when the page is shown; and plugins run some text. Dataview runs `` `= …` `` inline queries by default (its JavaScript queries are off by default, and its docs say they have access to the file system). Templater, when its trigger on new files is on (off by default, a setting on the person's machine), runs the `<%` commands in any new note of up to 100 KB outside its template and ignored folders. A plugin may run a code fence it claims, as Dataview runs `dataviewjs`. A capture's words copied into a wiki page could therefore hide text from the person reading it, call a server when the page opens, or run. The hook now refuses any change that would leave a wiki page holding one of these, anywhere on a line and in code too: HTML (a web or mail address in angle brackets stays a link), a `%%` comment, a Markdown image, an embed of a web address, a Templater command, a Dataview inline query (across a line break too), or a fence named other than one of 39 common languages. A character after a backslash shows as written, so `\%\%`, `\![` (a link then, not an image) and `&lt;b&gt;` pass, though `<%` never does, because Templater reads the raw file. It refuses a change it cannot read and any file in `wiki/` not ending `.md`, because a canvas or other file Obsidian opens could load from the web. `AGENTS.md` states the rule and its escapes as rule 6, and `/lint` lists pages that break it for the person. These are patterns the hook checks, not every way Obsidian or a plugin might read a page.

One independent review of the branch found three ways round the hook's first version, which judged only the lines a change added; each is now closed, with a test that failed on that version. That version assumed Claude Code refuses an edit whose old text is not in the file. Claude Code 2.1.304 instead matches it loosely, reading curly quotes as straight and decoding `\uXXXX` escapes (and then the new text too), and an edit that empties its text also takes the line break after it. So a tag written as `\u003cimg …\u003e`, or two clean lines joined into `Wow![x](…)`, passed. (This rests on the reviewer's reading of Claude Code's installed code, not on a live run.) Comparing lines as a multiset let the agent reuse a person's lines: replace the body of their `dataviewjs` block, write between their `%%` lines, or remove the fences around a tag they quoted. And a Dataview query split across a line break passed. The hook now refuses an edit whose old text is not in the page word for word, checks every text an emptied edit could leave, with and without the line breaks it may take, and judges the whole page, so a page that already holds a forbidden line, even the person's own, is theirs to fix. On main's hook five of the first version's fifteen tests failed, and on the first version four of the sixteen now; 22 deliberate breakages of the hook as it stands each turn a test red.

## The enclosing lasso (22 October)

**What Sam saw:** in a screen recording of his own Chrome, circling steps, a list or a picture took one small piece, so highlighting text and clicking the hand worked better. The lasso took only the passage under its loop's centre, and a loop more than 440 px across or down never closed.

**What a loop takes now:** what it goes round, in reading order, as one capture, its blocks a blank line apart. While it is drawn, the hand lights each line it will take, one box per line, so words a capture leaves out within a line (an `aria-hidden` span) light with it.

- Highlighted words under the loop's centre are taken as they are, and a picture or video at its centre as before.
- To find what it goes round, the lasso asks Chrome for the caret every 10 px down and about every 32 px across the loop, inside the window (at most 4,000 looks), and reads each block it finds as the pointer reads one: never a control, a field, navigation, the hand, or text the page's styles hide: not displayed, invisible, fully transparent by opacity, or a closed `details`' answer. Words clipped to a pixel for screen readers are left out and the sentence round them reads on without them. Rows laid out as blocks in the flow, as a result card's title and price are, or stacked in a flex column, read as lines of their own, for every grab. A box Chrome only reports as a block stays within its line, so the sentence round it stays whole: a floated drop cap, a positioned badge, the items of a flex row or a grid, and anything inside an inline box such as an `inline-flex` link or an `inline-block` formula. So rows a grid or a wrapping flex row stacks still run together, as they did before. A block too large to read whole is read a paragraph at a time, and no line is read twice. A loop reaching more than 120 blocks takes the first 120 and marks the capture cut short.
- A loop that reaches more than one block takes whole each block whose every line's start it holds, as a loop down a list's steps or round search results does. It holds a line's start when, along the top, middle and bottom of the line's first word, it reaches back into that word (or two lines' height into a short one), as an oval cutting a column's corners still does and the tip of one crossing a heading does not. Its rim crosses a block it holds words of only along one line, in the top or bottom quarter of the loop's height and away from its centre: of that block it takes only sentences on lines whose start it holds, so its bulge across the middle of a line above or below takes nothing. Every other block it reaches it reads as one loop alone would, so an oval round the middle of a paragraph, its rim over the first line of the next, takes the sentences it goes round.
- Within a block it takes each sentence it holds a quarter of the words of, two at least, each word clear of the rim by half its height, and everything between the first and last of them.
- A loop less than seven times its words' height tall (about five lines of 16 px text set 24 px apart) is drawn round a sentence: beyond the one at its centre it takes only sentences it holds whole, and a block whole only when it also encloses most of its words. So a small circle on one sentence still takes that sentence, and one in a search result's snippet does not take the address line above it, while a small loop round a short list takes each item.
- A loop holding no text takes the largest picture it mostly goes round.
- Loops up to 1,000 px across or down close; one flatter than 3:1 is still refused.
- A capture of several blocks links back with a text fragment running from the first four words of its first block's text to the last four of its last, so the link finds it even when a card's rows are separate blocks. Chrome matches each end within one line, so a link round code runs from its first line with words in it to its last, never to a lone closing brace, and Chrome opens it.
- While the page is busy reading the highlight, Chrome merges the pointer's moves into one event; the hand traces the merged moves after any gap of 40 ms or more, so a loop drawn meanwhile keeps its shape. Chrome withholds merged moves from pages not served securely (shown for page scripts, untested for the hand's own), so on an `http` page such a loop can still jump.
- With pictures off (the core build), a loop centred on a picture reads the text round it.
