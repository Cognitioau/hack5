# Current status and handoff

Updated 20 October 2026 AEDT. This is a working development build, not a completed production release or finished Yard submission.

## Implemented

- Version 0.2.0: universal browser activation with a one-time optional site-access grant, persistent tab/navigation/restart behavior, excluded sites and Pause everywhere. Chrome-protected pages remain unavailable.
- A toolbar popup holds everything that need not sit on the page: Grab selection, Grab page, Turn on everywhere / Pause everywhere, vault status (connected, or how many captures are kept on the device, with Connect vault), Settings, the graveyard and gesture help. Opening it grants activeTab; its grab wakes the active tab and asks the page to take its selection or the whole page, and the page's reason when nothing was taken (no selection, paused, a refused target) shows in the popup, which closes once a grab has begun. If Chrome's site-access prompt closes the popup mid-activation, the background finishes that activation when the grant arrives.
- Trusted Shift/Control circle/oval capture that forgives a loop stopping short (from about 0.83 of a turn), spiralling in or out, overshooting, wobbling or drawn as an egg, while still refusing a half circle or a scribble back and forth; sentence/selection preview with exact text highlights, source title/URL, early-release/typing/drag/focus cancellation and recoverable Spit back. Hovering alone never captures.
- One-click grabs: clicking the corner hand takes the current selection, otherwise the page (title, link and a 4,000-character excerpt of visible article/main reading text, skipping navigation, forms, dialogs, editable and hidden content). A settled selection grows a 30px chip at its end. Alt+Shift+Z takes a selection wherever the pointer rests, otherwise the pointed target.
- The page overlay is the hand and one bar: idle, a bare 36px hand; during a capture, a one-line status to its left (the hand sits at the right end, so it never moves), with only the buttons the moment needs (Cancel, Spit back, and Connect vault when no vault is connected). An error adds one short detail line ("Kept on this device. / Connect a vault to bury it in Obsidian.") and goes quiet after 12 seconds; saves go quiet after six. A related note has its own small toggle. There is no ⋯ menu, panel or receipt card any more, and a page can no longer pause the hand everywhere. At capture time one of three filmed zombie hands (generated footage matted onto the page, picked at random and never the same twice running) punches through the page below the target and slaps flat. It closes its fist on a paper copy of the words in the page's own type and colours (or the page peeled into a card), which tears free with a ragged edge, lifts and crumples into a creased ball in the fist, and yanks it under on a confirmed save or lets go on failure. After a yank the wall mends: filmed plaster flies back into the hole and its cracks close. The footage plays at 1.2× the speed it was filmed, and its wall (rim, plaster, dust, the marks the fingers leave) takes the page's own background colour, so a dark page gets dark plaster rather than a pale glow. The hand scales with the page's text size. Reduced motion shows a static grip; delayed saves fade the decoration within 3.6 seconds while the status stays truthfully queued.
- Atomic local capture/journal, serialized vault writes, create-only files, ETag metadata updates, attachment acknowledgements, lost-response reconciliation, visible failure states and bounded retries. With no vault connected a capture is kept on the device and its grab still plays out: the hand pulls the copy under and the page heals, while the status says it is kept on this device rather than in the vault.
- Packaged local MiniLM inference, persisted vectors, semantic/keyword retrieval and related-note ghosts. No AI provider key is required.
- Image bytes → verified visible crop → labelled link; explicit exact-origin download consent separate from universal page access, immediate revocation, no credential forwarding, size/type/redirect restrictions.
- YouTube confirmation-time moments, ad-clock rejection, partial native transcript reader, manual WebVTT/SRT/timestamped import, provenance and cue-specific search links.
- Recovery preserves pending local lifecycle changes, quarantines positively observed missing/edited evidence, and adopts verified newer remote lifecycle only when the local saved snapshot has not changed.
- Isolated local gaze calibration lab with explicit camera control, settled and steadiness-checked capture per point, per-session model choice by leave-one-target-out cross-validation, held-out diagnostics, a One Euro–smoothed live dot with a head-movement warning, a click-only diagnostics download with an offline evaluator, no automatic persistence and blocked external connections. Gaze does not yet arm page captures. Its tuning constants are unfitted starting points; see Gaze tuning below.
- Full and core build profiles, strict contracts, AST boundaries, pinned dependencies, public-source exclusions, license notices, CI and validated archive generation.

## Gaze tuning

**No human gaze accuracy has been measured.** This work makes the lab better founded and makes one live session produce the data to tune it. All constants live in `src/features/gaze/tuning.ts`:

- Collection: drop the first 350 ms after a target appears; capture once 8 consecutive frames keep the binocular iris position within a 0.03 eye-width range and that position lies at least 0.02 eye widths from where the eyes were in the target's first 150 ms, so a person still looking at the previous point is not captured there; reject frames beyond 3.5 robust deviations and average the rest; after 2.5 s, points that never became steady or never moved are captured and flagged rather than blocking. A shrinking centre dot is the fixation point; Click (or Space, since the captured target takes focus) enables only after capture. Optional auto-advance (default off) moves on 300 ms after capture.
- Model: leave-one-target-out cross-validation over variants iris-only, iris + quadratic, iris + head-rotation and all ten features, each with λ ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10} (targets weighted equally), choosing the simplest candidate within 10% of the best mean error; standardised inputs clipped to ±4.
- Live dot: One Euro filter in CSS pixels, min cutoff 0.6 Hz, β 0.0005, derivative cutoff 1 Hz, restart after a 500 ms gap. On synthetic white noise this leaves 0.40–0.48 of 40–120 px jitter and reaches 90% of a 600 px jump in ~320 ms.
- Movement warning: inter-eye distance ±12% or eye-centre shift > 0.5 inter-eye distances, cleared below 80% of each limit. Both are measured in the camera's normalised image coordinates, so on a 4:3 camera the shift limit is about a quarter tighter for vertical movement, and head roll beyond roughly 35° can trip the distance limit.
- Export and evaluation: **Download session diagnostics** (contents listed in [PRIVACY](PRIVACY.md)); `node scripts/gaze-eval.mjs <file…>` reprints per-variant cross-validation and check errors, an approximate pre-tuning reference, how steady and how far moved the live captures were, and settling, window-length and steadiness-range sweeps.

Synthetic evidence only: unit tests show the selection picks the quadratic variant for a quadratic map, iris-only when head features are noise, and iris + head when head turns explain the gaze; with the eye arriving 250, 750 or 1200 ms after each target appears, the synthetic check median is 3–7 px across three seeds, and stays 3–7 px with settling removed, because the steadiness range alone keeps reaction-period frames out of these step-like synthetic saccades (real saccades overshoot and drift, which settling still covers). Regression tests cover late arrival and a person who never leaves the previous point. On synthetic eyes, which never glance away after capture, the approximate pre-tuning reference with 12 averaged frames can match or beat the 8-frame window; the window sweep on real data should decide that.

Live inspection of one public YouTube watch page found its native transcript panel stuck on a spinner with no exposed cues. That is an extraction/availability limitation, not a claim that captions are absent. No broad native-transcript success rate is established; manual import is implemented and browser-tested.

## Review and fixes

Three explicitly authorized subagents owned capture/UI, vault/security, and toolchain/inference work. Independent review produced executable regressions for old probe authorization, settings-read races, late arming after revocation, media/vector acceptance inside IndexedDB callbacks, stale semantic search, interrupted import, missing alarms, future journal isolation and failed-index retry. Those confirmed failures were fixed before this handoff. The 0.2.0 review additionally exercised modifier/lasso false activation, hidden DOM selection, late ARM/archive replies, delayed activation prompts after pause, injection/revocation races, separate image consent and stale media results. An extra new-tab image regression found Chrome requires all-sites permission for visible crops without activeTab; the full profile now requests that optional grant, while core stays HTTP(S)-only. Actual injection remains HTTP(S)-only in both profiles. Tab/window changes now abort unfinished view work even when the original page is restored before a screenshot returns; six regressions cover late crops and delayed arming while retaining already recorded evidence. Confirmed issues were fixed with executable regressions. No Jev review or advisory service was used.

The 20 October review found and fixed three interaction defects, each with a regression that failed before the fix: Alt+Shift+Z ignored a live selection whenever the pointer rested on a control, form field, nav or the overlay; hovering the collapsed hand revealed its menu in-flow, sliding the hand out from under the cursor so the click opened the menu instead of grabbing; and Save page stored only the title. The previous CSS hand covered the passage it grabbed. The SVG rig that replaced it was rejected as robotic, so the hand became a Blender-rendered atlas, itself since replaced by footage (below). That rework found a pre-existing clip bug: the hole ellipse wound against the page rectangle, so the arm vanished in the back half of the hole. `graveClip` now winds both shapes the same way; its regression polygonises the clip and fails on the old flags. A second regression kept the forearm's cut end below the hole lip in every frame across four viewports; it failed on the first render and passed once the render reached further below the wrist. Both regressions retired with the rendered hand. An independent review of the rework found that, under reduced motion, an atlas decoding after the grab repainted the opening frame over the held fist; a regression failed on it before the fix. It also found the sender check was tested for ARM alone; that test now enumerates every page message and goes red if the atlas request skips the check.

The footage rework (also 20 October) replaced that atlas, whose motion fell short of a hand bursting out of the page as if from a grave. Three Seedance 2.5 takes are matted onto the page, and each grab plays one at random. The browser test's skin rule was retuned for the new hand and its debris, and it now films the whole grab: a single moment 640 ms after the click once caught a later hand still climbing out (2,679 hand pixels against a floor of 3,000). Its peak is 7,900 to 12,600 hand pixels across the three hands, and none outside the hand. Six deliberate breakages each turned the matching unit or security test red: a hand repeating, footage never drawn, every decoded frame kept, the hole allowed past the viewport foot, any number served as hand 0, and the yank skipped. With the footage never drawn, the browser test counted 0 hand pixels against its floor of 3,000. Filming the built extension then showed every selection grab leaving the page's own highlight around the taken words: Chrome paints a selection over the whole line box, and the patches covered only the glyphs (and not a trailing selected space, which the evidence range trims). Patches now cover the line box and the live selection's own fragments. Unit tests and a browser check that the highlight is gone failed before the fix; with it removed again, the browser check counted 2,482 highlight pixels against a limit of 30, and 0 after the fix in eight repeated runs.

The branch's independent review then reproduced, in the built extension, three selection shapes that still left the highlight showing: a long selection (the raw fragment list was capped at 64 in document order and not kept to the viewport), a selection across paragraphs (the line height came from the common ancestor, often `main` at `normal`), and a passage inside a translucent callout (the tint became see-through patches). It also found the fist closing at the viewport edge for a passage that runs off screen, and about 60 ms of page stall unpacking each hand's bytes. All five are fixed: the highlight is now measured per selected text node at its own line height, kept to the viewport and merged per line, tints are mixed over what lies beneath, the fist closes on the visible part, and the bytes unpack in an indexed loop. Undoing each fix turns its unit test red, and the browser checks for the three shapes counted 4,969 (glyph boxes only), 96 (no line-break cover) and 6,675 (tint unmixed) leftover highlight pixels against a limit of 30. A probe of 160 bold words selected from above the viewport went from 306,919 highlight pixels to 0.

Riding the build on his Retina Mac, Sam found the hand's movement a bit janky. Measured on the built extension at 2× (Playwright's headless Chromium 156 on this Mac's GPU, which runs at 60 Hz; 4 instrumented and 10 plain runs per build on white and on a #1d2126 page, interleaved), the page's main thread never dropped a frame in warm runs: the dark-page tint costs about 1.1 ms per footage frame (about 100 ms a grab, 0.3 ms on white), but the work never overran a frame. The jank was in what was drawn. The footage runs at 28.8 frames a second, so the picture changed on only 47–48% of display frames, each frame held two refreshes and every twelfth three (four or five at 120 Hz), and the hand stood still on 51–53% of display frames while it was moving. The first frame after the wall gave way was always blank, as its decode only started then, and the next one was on screen for a single refresh. Each display frame now mixes the footage frame with the next by how far the step has come (added at complementary strengths, an exact mix of colour and cover, so solid stays solid). The copy rides the interpolated grip, poses are taken at the display frame's own time, and the opening frames decode while the plaster cracks. Afterwards the picture changes on 99–100% of display frames, the moving hand is still on 0–2% of them, no burst frame is blank, and the main thread's time a grab is unchanged within run-to-run noise. In the fastest punch frames a mid-mix shows the fingers as a soft double image, like motion blur. Unit tests check the mix and its timing, the copy riding between frames, the early decode and the frames kept around a still fist (the first three failed on the old code; deliberate breakages of the timestamp, the additive mix, the early decode, the grip mix and frame pruning each turned a test red). All 449 automated tests passed (268 unit, 47 contract, 121 security, 13 browser). Not covered: a real 120 Hz display and real Chrome on a long-lived profile. Unexplained: both builds sporadically missed 1 to 9 refreshes in a row, in 2 of 40 old-build runs and 3 of 28 new-build runs. The longest came in each build's first launch: the old build's very first run, and the new build's first run just after its first mix. No main-thread task over 50 ms was recorded in any run, so these point to the GPU (perhaps compiling a pipeline on first use), not this code.
